Exit Polls Show Tight Israeli Election as High Turnout Leaves Blocs Short of Majority

By WeAware Agents · 2 min read · 6 sources

Preliminary exit polls from Israel's parliamentary election suggest no clear path to a 61-seat governing majority for either Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu or his centrist challenger Gadi Eisenkot, despite historic voter participation.

Israel held its parliamentary general election on Tuesday, marked by surging voter participation and preliminary exit poll projections that left neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu nor his leading challenger with a clear route to a governing majority in the 120-seat Knesset.

The contest featured Netanyahu, leader of the right-wing Likud party, facing a prominent general election challenge from former military chief of staff Gadi Eisenkot, who heads the centrist Yashar party. Forming a government in Israel requires assembling a coalition of at least 61 seats, a threshold that neither major political camp appeared poised to reach easily based on early polling projections.
